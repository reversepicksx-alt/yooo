Build a mandatory CAUSAL SCRIPT ENGINE into the Reverse Picks soccer model so recommendations are no longer created mainly from player averages, hit rates, trend color, projection-vs-line, or generic team strength. Treat every prop result as an OUTPUT produced by a football mechanism, and before allowing any prediction to become a pick, reconstruct the mechanism that would have to occur for that exact statistic to be generated. API-Sports already gives us enough raw information to infer these mechanisms automatically even though it does not provide a literal field called “high press” or “low block”: use the available API-Sports endpoints for fixtures and provider IDs, home/away and venue, recent fixtures, team season statistics, fixture team statistics, individual fixture player statistics, formations and starting XIs, substitutions/coaches, match events including goals/cards/red cards, current squads/roles, injuries/suspensions, head-to-head matches, odds/market favorite information, possession, total passes/pass accuracy, shots, shots on target, goalkeeper saves, corners, tackles and the other available match/player statistics. The engine must use those observed effects to infer the tactical workload rather than requiring a written tactical label. For every candidate prop, first define a stat-production chain, then inspect up to 20 recent matching-venue games for the target and up to 20 corresponding-venue games for the opponent, and most importantly build an OPPONENT-CREATED EXACT-ROLE WORKLOAD COHORT: identify players occupying the same real role as the target in recent matches against this opponent and determine whether the opponent consistently raises or suppresses that role’s workload compared with those players’ normal baselines. Do not merely compare averages; calculate opponent-role uplift/suppression and investigate the game conditions that created it. For example, for GK passes the chain is opponent territorial pressure/press shape → CB/pivot progression blocked or allowed → repeated resets to goalkeeper versus direct distribution → goalkeeper pass attempts; therefore an away goalkeeper can be a MORE even if his normal average is low when the opponent repeatedly forces keeper recycling. This is what the old model missed on Đorđe Petrović U24.5 away at Manchester City: projection said 23.5 and blindly selected Under, but the correct pregame causal audit should have tested how many pass attempts opposing goalkeepers receive at City relative to their normal away workload and whether City pressure forces repeated resets; that mechanism should have rejected the Under and potentially flipped it to More. For CB passes, the chain is team possession → opponent pressing/block geometry → availability of midfield progression → first-line recycling concentration → exact CB share; this is what should have caught Nahuel Ferraresi U50.5 at home: the old 46 projection ignored the possibility that Botafogo possession would concentrate through Ferraresi because Athletico allowed first-line circulation and Botafogo’s attacking structure could recycle sterile possession through the CBs; if exact-role CBs consistently receive a pass-volume uplift against Athletico, the Under must be rejected or flipped even when Ferraresi’s own historical average is lower. For pivot/CM passes, the chain is team possession → formation → single/double-pivot structure → opponent screen/press → whether progression routes through or bypasses the midfielder → minutes → pass attempts; this is what should have prevented Jon Moncayola O39.5 vs Levante from becoming a confident pick when the projection was only 40 against a 39.5 line, the current manager/formation was new, and it was uncertain whether possession would concentrate through Moncayola or be split among CBs, the other pivot, fullbacks and direct play; in a case like that the correct result is PASS / MODEL EDGE REJECTED, not automatically flip to Under. Apply equivalent causal chains to every prop type: clearances = opponent territory → progression channel → crosses/direct balls/box entries → target defender’s exact zone → defender forced to clear rather than intercept/retain → clearance count; goalkeeper saves = opponent attack volume → shot creation → shot accuracy → shots on target → shot quality/conversion → save opportunities, and do not treat total shots as saves evidence; shots = player role/position → team progression into his zone → touches/receptions → shot opportunity generation → attempts; shots on target = shot volume → shot locations/body orientation/role → accuracy → SOT; crosses = team width → opponent block/narrowness → wide progression → player receives in crossing zones → deliveries; attempted dribbles = isolation opportunities → transition/open-space frequency → defender matchup → player receives facing goal → take-on attempts; tackles = opponent progression through player’s defensive zone → role assignment → duel frequency → tackle opportunities; interceptions = passing lanes/pressing structure → opponent progression patterns → target’s screening zone → interception chances; fouls committed = defensive matchup → opponent dribble/contact frequency → recovery situations → foul opportunities; cards = foul exposure + opponent transition threat + tactical-foul situations + referee/card environment where available, while red-card-distorted historical games must never be treated as normal; assists/chances created = team possession/progression → player receives in creation zones → teammates make runs → final-ball attempts → conversion, so a high assist history without chance-creation volume is not enough; goals/fantasy score = combine the underlying causal components rather than treating fantasy score as a single stable stat, and explicitly model goal/assist binary-event risk because one scoring action can destroy an otherwise strong fantasy Under. Every historical match must pass a DISTORTION FILTER before being used: tag and downweight or remove red cards, early injuries, unusual substitutions, penalties, extreme 2+ goal game states, knockout desperation, formation anomalies, opponent rotations, short-minute appearances and any situation that materially changed normal workload. Never use an extreme stat performance as evidence until the engine checks why it happened. Separate player history from opponent-created workload: a player who is red in 8 of 10 can still be a MECHANISM EDGE MORE if this opponent reliably creates the exact workload for his role under clean game states, while a player green in 8 of 10 can still be rejected if tomorrow removes the mechanism that created those greens. For every match automatically classify the likely script when supported as CONTROL, SIEGE, SUPPRESSION, CHASE, TRANSITION, LOCK or combinations such as CONTROL→SIEGE, CONTROL→SUPPRESSION or CONTROL→LOCK, then estimate SCRIPT HALF-LIFE, meaning how long the expected tactical environment is likely to survive before a goal/red card/substitution forces a regime change. Run mandatory score-state branches before the final recommendation: target team scores first, opponent scores first, level around 60 minutes, target scores before 20 minutes, opponent scores before 20 minutes, and explicitly answer what the opponent does after the target scores first and whether the prop workload goes UP, DOWN, NEUTRAL or UNKNOWN and whether it FREEZES, SURVIVES or EXPLODES. Calculate venue-specific role effects instead of generic season averages, and compare the target’s normal same-venue workload against the exact-role workload opponents experience against today’s opponent; create metrics such as opponent_role_effect = workload_vs_opponent / same_players_normal_matching_venue_workload, but do not trust the ratio unless the comparable roles, minutes, formations and game states are valid. Current manager, current formation, current partnerships and last 3–5 matches under the current setup must outweigh stale history when clearly relevant. Never let a tiny numerical edge automatically become a recommendation: if projection is 40 vs 39.5 but causal role concentration is uncertain, output PASS. Never reverse a pick merely because a historical prediction lost; when backtesting these three misses, pretend the match has not happened and use only information available pregame so there is no result leakage or hindsight fitting. Final decision logic must reconcile the quantitative projection with exact role, venue, opponent-created workload, current regime, score states, market/control signal, distortion-adjusted history and strongest realistic opposite case; label the causal evidence CAUSAL CONFIRM, CAUSAL CONTRADICTION, MECHANISM EDGE, DISTORTED SAMPLE, or UNKNOWN and require a final explanation of exactly what football events must happen for the prop to hit, why this opponent is expected to create or suppress those events, what happened to genuine same-role players against this opponent, whether those comps were clean or distorted, and the exact tactical/score-state kill condition. The core principle of the entire engine is: DO NOT PREDICT THE NUMBER FROM THE NUMBERS; PREDICT THE FOOTBALL THAT GENERATES THE NUMBER, THEN USE API-SPORTS DATA TO TEST WHETHER THAT FOOTBALL REPEATS. API-Sports should therefore be treated as the mechanism-inference data source: it may not explicitly name every press/block/style, but its fixtures, formations, lineups, player workloads, possession/pass distributions, shots/SOT/saves, events, venue splits, H2H, team stats and score-state timelines are enough to observe what an opponent repeatedly makes each exact role do and automatically stop projection-only misses like Petrović Under, Ferraresi Under and the unjustifiably thin Moncayola Over from passing the final recommendation gate.
