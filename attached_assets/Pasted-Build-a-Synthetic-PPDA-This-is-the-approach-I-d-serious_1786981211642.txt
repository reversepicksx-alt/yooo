Build a “Synthetic PPDA”

This is the approach I’d seriously consider for Reverse Picks.

Something like:

Synthetic PPDA = Expected Opponent Passes / Effective Defensive Actions

Where:

Effective Defensive Actions

could be:

Tackles
+ Interceptions
+ Fouls
+ Possession Recoveries
+ Blocked Passes
+ Duel Wins

But don’t give each action equal weight.

For example

Interception       = 1.00
Tackle             = 0.90
Blocked pass       = 0.75
Possession recovery= 0.70
Duel won           = 0.45
Foul               = 0.35

Those weights should eventually be learned from historical data, rather than chosen arbitrarily.

That’s where your model gets interesting.

Even better: don’t try to replicate PPDA

I’d actually make a Pressing Intensity Score (PIS).

Something like:

PIS = f(DA rate, opponent pass suppression, recoveries, tackles, interceptions, possession, territory, game state)

Then normalize it to:

0–100

For example:

PIS	Interpretation
90–100	Extreme press
75–89	Very aggressive
60–74	Aggressive
45–59	Moderate
30–44	Passive
0–29	Deep/low block

This gives your Game Simulator a much more useful variable than a single PPDA number.

And there’s one huge upgrade

You can create separate pressing phases.

Instead of:

Team PPDA = 8.4

your bot could determine:

HIGH PRESS       82/100
MID-BLOCK        61/100
LOW BLOCK        34/100
COUNTERPRESS     76/100

Then feed those into your Bayesian model.

For example:

Team a

High press:       86
Counterpress:     81
Mid-block:        63
Low block:        28

Versus team b

High press:       41
Counterpress:     38
Mid-block:        72
Low block:        84

That tells your simulator how the teams defend, not merely what their PPDA happened to be.

And that’s especially useful for predicting things like possession, shots, passes, turnovers, corners, etc.

The fundamental limitation is that without event coordinates you can’t perfectly reconstruct location-based PPDA; however, API-Sports gives enough aggregate information to construct a proxy/synthetic pressing metric.
