1. Zero-Inflated Models for Rare Count Stats
In Stage 6, you use a Gamma-Poisson mixture (Negative Binomial) for count stats like goals and cards. However, soccer has heavy structural zeros (e.g., a defender who physically never pushes up for a corner kick). Standard Negative Binomial models underestimate the probability of zero in these cases.
You should implement a Zero-Inflated Poisson (ZIP) or Zero-Inflated Negative Binomial (ZINB) model. This splits the prediction into two processes: the probability of a structural zero, and the count distribution if not zero.

2. Implementation of Global Elo Ratings
In Stage 6, your covariate adjustment for opponent strength relies heavily on season concession averages. This falls apart early in the season or across different leagues (e.g., Champions League or World Cup).
Integrate a global club/international Elo rating system. Instead of raw concession averages, adjust the expected baseline based on the Elo delta between the two teams. This provides an immediate, highly accurate opponent strength modifier even when n=0 for head-to-head data.
3. Dynamic Score-State Probability (Game Scripting)
You have a robust pre-match situational engine (Stage 7), but possession and volume stats (passes, tackles) are heavily dictated by in-game score states. A team that goes up 1-0 in the 15th minute will naturally drop possession, impacting all passing props.
 Calculate the expected goal spread (from odds or Elo).
 Map the spread to expected minutes spent leading, tied, or trailing.
 Apply your CDM inversion and deep-block modifiers dynamically based on the expected time spent in these game states, rather than a static 90-minute assumption.
